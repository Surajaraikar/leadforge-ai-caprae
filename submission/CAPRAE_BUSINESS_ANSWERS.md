# Caprae Capital — Business Questions

Prepared for: Suraj Raikar
Role: Full Stack Developer

## 1. What is Caprae's Mission?

Caprae Capital’s mission is to make others great through entrepreneurship. In practice, I understand that as helping searchers, acquisition entrepreneurs, founders, and private-equity teams create durable value by combining operator experience, strategic capital, and practical execution support. Caprae presents itself as an end-to-end partner across sourcing, diligence, transaction support, and post-acquisition growth—not simply a provider of leads or financial advice.

What stands out is the founder/operator orientation behind that mission. Caprae’s public materials emphasize that its team has built, acquired, and operated companies and that support continues into the first 100 days and longer-term value-creation work. That makes the mission concrete: help capable entrepreneurs find the right businesses, make better-informed acquisition decisions, and operate those businesses more effectively after close.

Technology supports the mission when it reduces repetitive work and improves decision quality without obscuring accountability. A useful system should make source data traceable, surface uncertainty, and let an operator focus on the conversations and judgments that matter. That principle shaped LeadForge AI: it prioritizes real records, visible scoring criteria, source provenance, and honest missing-data handling.

The Full Stack Developer role would let me deepen those skills while contributing to systems whose value can be measured in better sourcing capacity, faster analysis, and more repeatable execution.

## 3. How is Caprae Changing the ETA Space and Broader PE?

Caprae is changing entrepreneurship through acquisition by packaging work that is often fragmented across list builders, outreach teams, analysts, consultants, and post-close advisers into an integrated operator-led service. Its public service model spans defining the acquisition criteria, sourcing off-market owners, qualifying opportunities, supporting preliminary diligence, and helping an owner build the first-100-day and longer-term value-creation plan. That can give searchers and smaller PE teams access to capabilities they might not be able to hire individually.

The broader PE impact is the combination of that service model with technology and automation. Structured sourcing systems can expand proprietary deal flow; consistent data-quality and qualification rules can focus analyst attention; and AI-readiness work can identify portfolio-company opportunities with a realistic payback. The important difference is that technology is tied to a buy box, diligence question, operating KPI, or value-creation plan rather than treated as a standalone novelty.

I see the opportunity for a Full Stack Developer in making those workflows repeatable and auditable. Products can connect research, sourcing, outreach, diligence, and portfolio operations while preserving provenance and human review. Done well, that improves speed and capacity without pretending software replaces investor or operator judgment—and gives founder-led, lower-middle-market teams tools that were previously easier for much larger firms to afford.
